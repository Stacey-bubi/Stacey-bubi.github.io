## Why binary contrasts were never enough

The [earlier formalisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03658) defines a concept direction
from counterfactual pairs: queen minus king, woman minus man. It is precise and it works,
but it needs the concept to have a natural opposite.

Most concepts do not. There is no natural counterfactual for "bird". You can contrast it
with "mammal", but that is a choice among several, and different choices give different
directions. The formalism had nothing to say about the majority of what a model knows.

## Polytopes instead of directions

The fix is to stop asking for a direction and start asking for a shape.

A categorical concept with several mutually exclusive values is represented as a
**polytope** — for $k$ values, a simplex — with each value occupying a vertex. A binary
concept is then the degenerate case: two vertices, and the line between them is the
familiar direction.

Then the hierarchical result. Semantic containment between concepts corresponds to a
specific geometric relationship between their representations. The taxonomy is not just
metadata about the concepts; it is present in the arrangement of the vectors.

This is worth pausing on. The model was trained to predict the next token. Nothing in that
objective requires the WordNet hierarchy to be recoverable from the geometry of its
vocabulary space, and yet it is.

## The pattern I keep noticing

Two papers on this list, from unrelated directions, both replace a line with a region.

Here, a categorical concept is a simplex rather than a direction. In
[Concept Cones](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17420), refusal is a cone rather than a
direction. The first is a statement about how meaning is organised, the second about how
behaviour is mediated, and they were not written in conversation with each other.

If a concept generally occupies a region, then any method whose whole plan is to find *the*
direction and remove it is working with the wrong shape from the start. That is not fatal —
removing a line from a region still does something — but it does explain why single
directions keep turning out to be incomplete.
