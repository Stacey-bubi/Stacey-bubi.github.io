## What difference-in-means was missing

Difference-in-means reports where two clouds of activations differ on average. It has no
notion of what makes a direction good, so a sweep over layers and positions has to pick
whichever candidate happens to score best after the fact.

RDO defines what good means and optimises for it directly, with three requirements
applied at once rather than checked afterwards:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{abl}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ablation}}
+ \lambda_{\text{add}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{addition}}
+ \lambda_{\text{ret}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{retain}}
$$

with weights $1.0$, $0.2$ and $1.0$. The retain term is the one difference-in-means has no
analogue for: it explicitly penalises changing behaviour on harmless prompts. That is why
the resulting direction damages TruthfulQA less.

## From a direction to a cone

The larger move is optimising a set. Instead of one vector, RCO trains an orthonormal
basis $B = [b_1, \dots, b_N]$ and studies the region

$$
\Big\{ \textstyle\sum_i \lambda_i b_i \;\Big|\; \lambda_i \ge 0 \Big\}
$$

requiring that vectors sampled from inside it all mediate refusal. The sampling detail is
easy to skip but matters: they sample uniformly over the non-negative part of the unit
sphere. Sampling convex combinations of the basis vectors instead would concentrate mass
near the corners and would not test the interior.

They find such cones up to five dimensions, in every model.

## Orthogonality is not independence

This is the part I keep coming back to.

Find two refusal directions, observe that they are orthogonal, and the natural conclusion
is that they are separate mechanisms. The paper shows that conclusion does not follow. They
take a direction strictly orthogonal to the difference-in-means direction, ablate it, and
watch the other one. In the later layers its expression in the activations drops anyway.

The reason is straightforward once said out loud. Orthogonality is a statement about two
vectors at a single point in the network. Between that point and the output sit attention
and MLP layers, which are not linear. A right angle at one layer says nothing about what a
non-linear function does downstream.

So they define the stronger property. Two directions $\lambda, \mu$ are
**representationally independent** if ablating one leaves the cosine similarity of the
activations with the other unchanged at every layer. Enforcing it costs attack strength —
the constrained directions are weaker than the unconstrained ones — but at least five
independent directions survive in Gemma-2-2B, each still well above a random vector.

Then they show these are reachable from the input. A GCG suffix optimised against one of
them jailbreaks the model 36% of the time. The independent directions are not a latent
curiosity; the model is using them.

## Why this matters beyond refusal

Take the two results together and a lot of defensive work looks shaky.

If a concept occupies a cone rather than a line, removing a rank-one subspace is not
removing the concept. And if orthogonality does not certify independence, then every method
that treats orthogonality as evidence of having isolated a concept is proving less than it
claims.

That second point lands on
[Safety Subspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14185), which measures subspace overlap and
concludes that safety is not linearly separable from general capability. Its metric treats
orthogonality as what separation would look like. But by this paper's argument, even a
perfectly orthogonal result would not have established independence. So the conclusion
survives and gets stronger: subspace defences fail on two counts rather than one.
