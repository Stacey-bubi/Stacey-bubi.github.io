## Two spaces, not one

The first thing this paper clears up is that "the representation space" is two different
things.

**The unembedding space $\Gamma$** holds the fixed vocabulary vectors in the output
matrix — the model's static dictionary of what each token means to it.

**The embedding space $\Lambda$** holds the dynamic activations flowing through the layers
as a specific prompt is processed.

A concept can be linearly represented in either. They are related but they do not share a
coordinate system, and treating a direction found in one as directly usable in the other is
a mistake this paper is largely about fixing.

Theorem 2.2 connects them. The model's preference between two words — the logit gap — is
determined by the inner product $\lambda^\top \bar{\gamma}_W$ between the current context
vector and the concept direction taken from the vocabulary matrix. This is why linear
probing works at all: the model itself is performing a linear measurement of how well its
current state aligns with fixed directions in its dictionary.

The concept direction $\bar{\gamma}_W$ is defined from counterfactual pairs — queen minus
king, woman minus man — averaged. That definition is precise enough to prove things with,
which is what the rest of the paper does.

## The broken ruler

Here is the problem the paper is really about.

The model is trained only to produce correct output probabilities through a softmax. Nothing
in that objective constrains the geometry of the internal space. It can be stretched along
some axes and squashed along others with no effect on the loss. This is
**unidentifiability**, and it means Euclidean tools are not trustworthy by default:

Nothing rewards keeping unrelated concepts orthogonal, so two concepts can appear aligned
purely because the space is compressed in that region. A concept may live along a direction
whose scale plain Euclidean distance gets wrong. And the static dictionary and the dynamic
activations may follow different coordinate systems entirely.

Applying a straight ruler to a stretched space gives confident, wrong answers about how
concepts relate.

## The causal inner product

The fix is to replace the ruler. Define

$$
\langle \bar{\gamma}, \bar{\gamma}' \rangle_C
= \bar{\gamma}^\top \operatorname{Cov}(\gamma)^{-1} \bar{\gamma}'
$$

which is whitening by the covariance of the vocabulary vectors. Under this inner product,
causally independent concepts come out orthogonal — which is what you wanted "orthogonal"
to mean in the first place.

The same transformation converts a concept direction into an intervention:

$$
\bar{\lambda}_W = \operatorname{Cov}(\gamma)^{-1} \bar{\gamma}_W
$$

Lemma 2.4 gives the property that makes this a steering vector rather than a blunt
instrument: $\bar{\lambda}_W^\top \bar{\gamma}_Z = 0$ for every causally independent concept
$Z$, while $\bar{\lambda}_W^\top \bar{\gamma}_W > 0$. It moves what you targeted and leaves
the independent ones alone.

Tested across 27 concepts on LLaMA-2-7B and Gemma-2B, with one result worth carrying: on
LLaMA-2 the plain Euclidean inner product works unexpectedly well, close to the causal one.
On Gemma-2B it does not. Whether you can get away with Euclidean geometry depends on the
model, and you cannot tell which case you are in without checking — which is worth
remembering before assuming any published cosine-similarity result transfers between models.

## Why this sits next to LEACE

[LEACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819) whitens activations by their inverse covariance,
projects, and unwhitens. This paper whitens vocabulary vectors by their inverse covariance
and calls the result the correct inner product.

Different subfields, different notation, same correction: the raw space is distorted, so
undo the distortion before doing geometry in it. Both are responses to the anisotropy
[Ethayarajh measured in 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1006/). Two papers converging on
the same fix from unrelated directions is a decent sign it is the right one.

## Where I think it is weak

The key assumption is about a **uniformly sampled vocabulary**, not about real text. In
actual usage, causally independent concepts are often correlated, and that is exactly where
the guarantee is weakest.

The inner product is also not uniquely identified — $d$ free parameters remain, and the
specific choice is made for convenience rather than derived. The empirical work is narrow
too: 27 concepts, all single-token, one main model.

The gap I care about most is that this works in the **input and output spaces only**.
Refusal directions and steering vectors live in the intermediate layers, which are not
covered here. Nobody has yet redone the abliteration analysis under a properly chosen inner
product, and I would like to see it.
