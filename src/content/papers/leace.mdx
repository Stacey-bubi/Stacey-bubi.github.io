## The two conditions

LEACE asks for a transformation of the activations $X$ satisfying two things at once.

**Erasure.** The cross-covariance between the transformed activations and the concept label
$Z$ must be exactly zero:

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(PX + b,\, Z) = 0
$$

Zero cross-covariance is what makes every linear classifier useless. A linear classifier
reads a linear relationship, and there is no longer one to read. This is a much stronger
claim than the accuracy drops most erasure papers report — it is not that a particular probe
fails, it is that no linear probe can succeed.

**Minimal damage.** Among all transformations meeting that constraint, take the one that
moves the activations least:

$$
\min \; \mathbb{E}\big[\lVert PX + b - X \rVert^2_M\big]
$$

The solution has a closed form, so there is nothing to train and no adversarial game to
play. That is also where the roughly two orders of magnitude speedup over RLACE, the
gradient-based alternative, comes from.

## Why whitening is the whole point

Here is the part that connects this paper to everything else on the list.

The obvious way to remove a direction is orthogonal projection: find the concept direction,
subtract its component along that axis, done. That is what
[abliteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717) does, and what INLP did before it.

Orthogonal projection assumes the space is round. It is not. Contextual representations are
[anisotropic](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1006/): the vectors occupy a narrow cone instead
of filling the space, and a few rogue dimensions carry variance far out of proportion to the
rest. In a space stretched like that, a step that is orthogonal in coordinates is a large
step along a high-variance axis in practice. That is where collateral damage comes from.

LEACE handles it directly:

1. Take the covariance of the activations, $\Sigma_{XX}$.
2. **Whiten**, compressing the stretched cone into a sphere where variance is equal in
   every direction and features are uncorrelated.
3. Project *there*, where orthogonality means what you want it to mean.
4. **Unwhiten**, so the following layers do not receive a distribution they have never seen.

The projection is the easy part. Choosing the coordinate system to project in is the paper.

The cost of getting that wrong is measurable. Erasing gender from BERT embeddings, LEACE
destroys **17 dimensions** of the representation where INLP — projecting orthogonally and
iteratively — destroys **360**, for the same guarantee. That single comparison is the whole
argument for oblique over orthogonal projection. Gender classification falls to chance
either way, TPR-GAP drops from 0.198 to 0.084, and profession prediction barely moves,
77.3% against 79.3% untouched.

## Rediscovery, and why one layer is never enough

Erase the concept at layer 5, probe at layer 12, and it is back.

The information is redundant enough across the residual stream that later computation
rebuilds it from correlated features left in place. So LEACE has to be applied at every
layer in sequence, each layer's output feeding the next, and only then is the concept gone
from the model rather than from one slice of it.

I find this more useful than the erasure guarantee itself. It says the linear direction you
found was one copy among several — which should make anyone nervous about rank-one edits,
and lines up with the [cone](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17420) result from a different
direction entirely.

## Where I think it is weak

The guarantee is **linear only**. It says nothing about a deep MLP probe, though in practice
linear erasure does seem to hurt non-linear probes too.

More importantly, **collateral damage is measured as how far the internal vectors moved**,
not as behaviour. The authors flag this themselves and say behavioural metrics would be the
better test. Moving a vector a short distance and keeping a model useful are not the same
claim. Relatedly: a classifier can no longer read gender off the embeddings, but whether the
model still *behaves* in a biased way in harder settings is a different question, and this
does not answer it.

## What I want to do with it

The obvious experiment, which the paper does not run: use LEACE instead of orthogonal
projection to remove a refusal direction.

Abliteration's known cost is that the model gets worse at unrelated things — Qwen2.5-14B
loses 7.5 points of TruthfulQA. If that damage is geometric, caused by projecting in a
distorted space, whitening first should reduce it. Either it does, which says the damage was
an artefact of the metric, or it does not, which says refusal and capability are genuinely
entangled and no amount of careful projection separates them. That second answer is roughly
what [Safety Subspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14185) argues, and it would be the more
interesting result.
