## The claim

Out-of-context reasoning is the observation that a fine-tuned model sometimes generalises much
further than the fine-tuning data should support. It has been treated as evidence of something
structured going on inside.

This paper offers a duller explanation for many of the reported cases. LoRA fine-tuning ends up
adding what amounts to a constant steering vector to the activations, pushing the model toward
some general concept. That single push helps on the fine-tuning task and on every other task
related to that concept, which is what the surprising generalisation consists of.

## Why the backdoor case is the interesting one

A backdoor is the textbook example of conditional computation: behave normally, unless the trigger
appears.

If an unconditional constant vector reproduces that, the conditionality is not stored as a rule the
model checks. It emerges from how a constant push interacts with context — the same nudge only
changes the output when the surrounding context makes that output plausible.

That is a genuinely different mental model of what fine-tuning does, and a more tractable one.

## What it means for the rest of this list

If fine-tuning mostly moves models along a small number of directions, then two things usually
discussed separately are closer than they look.
[Abliteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717) edits activations or weights along a direction.
Fine-tuning attacks retrain the model. If the retraining is largely equivalent to adding a
direction, the two attack families overlap heavily, and a defence against one has a chance against
the other.

It also gives a mechanism for [Safety Subspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14185). If fine-tuning
is a push along a few directions, and safety occupies directions overlapping with general
capability, then harmless fine-tuning breaking alignment is exactly what you would predict.

The general lesson is the one I want to keep: check whether a constant offset explains the
behaviour before concluding the model learned something clever.

## How far to trust it

It is a workshop paper, so the scope is limited relative to the claim's reach. "Many instances of
out-of-context reasoning in the literature" is not all of them — this shows a simple mechanism
suffices in the cases examined, not that no case requires more.

And it is about LoRA specifically. Low-rank adapters are structurally predisposed toward low-rank,
steering-like effects, so how far this extends to full fine-tuning is a fair question.
