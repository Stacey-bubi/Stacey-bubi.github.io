## Why abliteration needs short refusals

[Abliteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717) finds its direction by averaging
activations on harmful prompts, averaging on harmless ones, and subtracting. That works best
when the two sets differ in a consistent, concentrated way.

Templated refusals give it exactly that. Every refusal starts with much the same handful of
tokens, so the difference between the two activation means is sharp, low-dimensional and
easy to locate.

The attack's requirement is not that refusal exists. It is that refusal is *concentrated*.

The conceptual basis is Qi et al. (2024) on shallow alignment: safety behaviour in aligned
models sits in the first few generated tokens. Extended refusal deliberately delays and
stretches the refusal so that concentration never forms.

## The defence, and what it buys

Retrain so a refusal has three parts: a neutral explanation of the topic, the explicit
refusal, and an ethical justification of why the request is unacceptable. Now the
safety-relevant content is spread across many tokens and many dimensions. Difference-in-means
still returns *a* direction, but no single direction carries the behaviour.

The numbers are stark. Baseline models collapse under abliteration — Llama-2-7B falls from
100% to 20.7% refusals, Qwen2.5-3B from 93.1% to 15.1%, Qwen2.5-1.5B from 93.8% to 13.6%,
a 70–80 point drop. The extended-refusal versions hold above 90%, with a worst case of about
9 points lost. MMLU and perplexity are broadly preserved, so this is not bought by making the
model worse.

The training set is about 4,300 harmful prompts from BeaverTails, AdvBench, StrongReject and
TDC-2023, with the extended refusals generated by GPT-4o, mixed with roughly 5,700 harmless
instruction-response pairs from Alpaca-GPT4 to keep ordinary usefulness intact.

## Why the coherence constraint matters

It would be easy to fake these results. Pick the direction that most reduces refusals and you
can usually find one that reduces them to zero by destroying the model — it stops refusing
because it has stopped making sense.

Here the attack only counts a direction if coherence stays at or above 70%, so the model must
still produce usable text after the edit. Any evaluation of an abliteration defence without a
constraint like this should be treated with suspicion.

## What I like about it

It does not fight the linear representation hypothesis, it works around it. Abliteration needs
the concept to be localised; make it not localised and the attack loses its precondition.
That is a more robust place to stand than arguing about whether the direction is findable.

It should also compose with other defences rather than replace them, since it only changes
what the refusal looks like.

## Where I would push on it

The evaluation attacks the defended model with single-direction ablation — the attack the
defence was designed against. That is the weakest form of evaluation.

The interesting question is what happens under
[Refusal Cone Optimisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17420) fitted directly against the
extended-refusal model. If the safety signal really is spread across many dimensions, a
cone-based attack should need a higher-dimensional cone to remove it — a much stronger
result than the one reported, and a quantitative one. If a five-dimensional cone still strips
it out, the defence is buying less than it appears to. Either answer is worth knowing, and
the experiment is not expensive.

Beyond that: models up to 7B only, so transfer to larger models is untested. Refusals get
long, and there is a real usability cost to answering every borderline request with a
three-part essay that goes unquantified here. And since the extended refusals were generated
by GPT-4o, the defence inherits whatever that model's refusal style and judgement bring with
them.
